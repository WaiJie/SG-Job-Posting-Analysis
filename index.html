              Key findings

              Salary is driven mainly by role and experience. Experience pays most in the first few years (about 9% per year for years 0-2), then levels off after 10 years. Seniority adds up to +48% (Senior Management vs Executive). Tools like Python, Go, and C++ carry a premium, though tool effects are smaller than role and experience. The model explains about 68% of salary differences between jobs.

                  Sample

                  The model starts with 421,355 postings. After keeping one record per job (248,437), filtering to realistic salaries (SGD 500-30,000 midpoint) and experience (0-40 years), and removing jobs without a role, 214,138 unique postings remain. The predicted salary is the midpoint of the advertised range.

                  Features

                  Role (with Software Engineer as the baseline) + experience (curved to capture fast early growth that slows later) + 53 key tools + seniority level (8 levels) + employment type (7 types) + location district. Month, recruiter flags, and 47 tools that did not pass the false-discovery-rate test are excluded.

                  Estimation

                  The model uses a standard regression on log salary. Performance is measured on a 20% holdout of unseen employers: mean absolute error (MAE) of about SGD 1,191 and an R squared of 0.683. Tools were selected using a method that controls for false positives across 100 tests.

                  Caveats

                  These are associations, not proof of cause. Only postings with advertised salaries are included. Tool data covers about half of postings. Role, tools, and experience overlap, so each effect is measured after adjusting for the others. Only 4 months of data (Jun-Sep 2026) are covered. Salary estimates beyond 12 years of experience are less reliable.

                  Limitations

                  What the data can and cannot tell you.

                  Early release

                  This preview focuses on job-posting activity and role analysis. Additional dimensions may be added in future releases.

                  Observed postings, not hires

                  Posting counts measure observable job-posting activity. They do not directly measure hires, vacancies filled, or employment outcomes.

                  Postings are not always unique jobs

                  Jobs may be reposted, duplicated, or listed in multiple places. Observed posting volume may therefore differ from the number of unique hiring needs.

                  Role classification is provisional

                  Roles are extracted and grouped using local large language models and an experimental taxonomy. Some postings may be assigned to neighbouring or incorrect roles, particularly where occupations share similar skills and responsibilities. Role-level results may change in future releases.

                  Recruiter postings affect the signal

                  Recruitment agencies may post on behalf of other employers and can contribute substantially to observed volume. The recruiter filter is provided to make this effect visible.

                  Limited observation window

                  This release covers June–September 2026 and reflects only observable postings during this period. It should not be interpreted as a complete picture of the Singapore labour market. June 2026 is incomplete: duplicate job posts were not collected at the start of the window, so June volumes are understated.

                  Pay and experience are indicative

                  Salary figures use advertised monthly basic pay where stated, kept to SGD 500–30,000, reported as 25th percentile / median / 75th percentile of the minimum, midpoint and maximum advertised. Experience comes from the stated minimum years required, kept to 0–40 years. Postings without these fields are excluded, so pay and experience counts are smaller than posting counts. Postings can carry several job functions, so job-function figures count a posting once per tag and can exceed unique postings.
